Rights-based revenue without giving up core control.

What Can Be Licensed

Categories of monetizable rights.

Availability depends on the asset, stage, and strategic fit. The point is controlled monetization, not broad indiscriminate access.

Brand and identity systems

Selected marks, creative systems, and brand expressions that fit the long-term positioning of the portfolio.

Operating frameworks

Selected system logic, workflow structures, or controlled methods that can be used under defined commercial terms.

Digital property and content

Rights tied to digital assets, domains, or reusable materials when the use case supports the broader asset strategy.

Good Fit

The strongest licensing requests tend to share the same traits.

Selective licensing works best when the request is defined well enough to evaluate scope, standards, and recurring commercial value.

Fit Signal

Clear use case

The partner can explain exactly what will be used, how it will be used, and why the asset fits the opportunity.

Fit Signal

Defined market scope

Territory, channel, category, or audience is specific enough to evaluate exclusivity, standards, and conflicts.

Fit Signal

Operational readiness

The request includes a credible execution path, not just broad interest without distribution, production, or delivery capability.

Fit Signal

Rights discipline

The partner understands that licensing is controlled use under standards and terms, not unrestricted ownership transfer.

Collaboration Models

Deal structures built for selective leverage.

Licensing can support multiple kinds of commercial relationships as long as the ownership and use terms remain clear.

Brand Collaboration

Campaigns, products, and co-branded programs

Structured brand use for aligned launches, capsules, or market-specific collaboration opportunities.

Channel Rights

Distribution, territory, or category-specific use

Selective rights built around scope, territory, quality standards, and commercial performance expectations.

Systems Use

Operational or framework licensing

Defined use of selected internal methods or structures when that arrangement creates strategic and commercial alignment.

Partnership Path

Strategic relationship first, license second

Some licensing discussions begin as broader partnerships before a specific rights package is scoped.

What Usually Does Not Fit

Licensing is selective by design.

Requests are less likely to advance when they are broad, undefined, or inconsistent with the retained-control model.

  • Open-ended use with no scope Requests that do not define channel, territory, standards, or term are difficult to evaluate and usually do not progress.
  • Full control transfer of core assets Branch Off Holdings is structured around retained ownership, not routine sale of core brand or systems control.
  • Speculative ideas without execution capacity Interest alone is not enough if there is no operating path, distribution plan, or credible commercial implementation.
  • Generic consulting framed as licensing Licensing discussions should involve defined rights use, not loosely scoped advisory requests with no asset-use component.
